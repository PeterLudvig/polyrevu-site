About

How PolyRevu scores

We read what people have publicly written and shown about a place, and turn it into scores you can inspect. A stated method and visible gaps are what make a score worth trusting — so here is what a score means, what we read, what we hold back, and where we don't have coverage yet.

The score

What a score means

A PolyScore is a synthesis, not one reviewer's opinion. It says where opinion about something sits on a sentiment scale from 0 to 100.

0 — everything we read leaned negative100 — it all leaned positive

It describes how opinion leans, not how big, famous or expensive a place is. Beside it we show:

  • Agreement — how much the sources agree with one another. A mixed picture is shown as mixed, never smoothed over.
  • Source breadth — how many kinds of source stand behind a score, so one built on a wide base is distinguishable from a thin one.

No ads influence any of it; the ranking is the score.

More than one number

Scores for the things you actually ask about

A single number for a whole restaurant hides most of what people say. So a place is scored at several levels:

The place overall
one score for the venue as a whole
Four categories
food, service, atmosphere and price
Specific things
a dish, a drink, the patio, the wait — whatever people actually discuss
Their qualities
the crust of the pizza, the speed of the service

Specific things roll up into general ones: remarks about the crust and the toppings count toward the pizza, and the pizza counts toward the food. You can ask the general question or the specific one and see what the answer rests on.

What we read

The sources we read

We synthesize across several kinds of public source, and keep them apart so you can see what each one says.

Reviews
mainstream reviewer volume at scale
Editorial
professional critics and review desks
Blogs
independent and enthusiast writing
Forums
community discussion and local knowledge
Photos
what the room and the plates actually look like
Video
walkthroughs and first-person visits

From each source we keep short structured notes: which place, what was discussed, and whether the remark leaned favourable or not. We do not keep copies of other people's pages, and we do not republish their words or images. We name sources as provenance.

What we hold back

When we don't publish a score

  • One voice is not a consensus. A score is published only when independent sources stand behind it. Until then it is held, and we say so.
  • Serious claims need corroboration. A remark about health, safety or conduct is not shown on the strength of a single report.
  • The right place, or nothing. A passage that may be about the restaurant next door is held back rather than counted.

Honest gaps

Where we don't have coverage yet

A score is only as good as what we were able to read, so we say where we fall short rather than imply coverage we don't have.

  • We're focused on New York City to start, and we're expanding outward from there.
  • A newer or quieter venue may not yet have enough sources for a confident read — when that's the case, the source-breadth signal shows it instead of hiding it.
  • Some categories and neighborhoods are better covered than others today; the holdouts shrink as we read more.

Visible holdouts are part of the method, not a footnote.

For site owners

Our crawler

If you run a website, you may see requests from our crawler. It identifies itself, obeys robots.txt, and can be turned away with one line. What it reads, how it behaves and how to opt out are on the crawler notice page.

The origin

Where PolyRevu started

PolyRevu started on a block in Chelsea. The problem was ordinary: too many opinions scattered across too many places, and no honest way to reconcile them into a decision you could trust.

So we built the thing we wanted — something that reads widely, reasons over what it finds, and shows its work.
